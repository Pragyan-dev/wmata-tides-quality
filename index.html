How the filters work

Loose keeps every inferred event. Moderate requires gaps of at most 120 seconds, at most 20% off-route pings, and at least 10 on-route pings. Strict also requires complete trips inside the window, shorter gaps, shape-based times, and no flagged speed, stale ping, or coverage issues.

Spacing between buses

A headway is the time between bus departures. We compare buses scheduled one after the other at the same stop, on the same route, and in the same direction.

How the spacing metrics work

For frequent service, the limit is the scheduled gap plus 15%, rounded up to the next whole minute. A gap can be short enough to count as bunching and still be below the limit.

To get the typical gap, we divide each measured gap by its scheduled gap and take the middle result.

Methodology and limitations

From pings to events

The pipeline converts WMATA GTFS-Realtime observations to TIDES tables. It projects vehicle locations onto route shapes and interpolates stop events between pings.

Schedule adherence

Timepoint departures count as on time from 2 minutes early to 7 minutes late. The last timepoint uses arrival, with a 7-minute late bound. Coverage is the share of represented timepoints with an inferred event.

What the feed misses

A public real-time feed is a stand-in for an agency's internal AVL data, which is what TIDES is designed for. Missing pings, GPS drift and the collection boundaries can affect the estimates.

Spacing between buses

Bunching means a gap under 2 minutes between consecutive scheduled buses at the same route, direction and timepoint, including overtaking. Frequent service has scheduled gaps of 12 minutes or less.
